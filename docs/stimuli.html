Stimuli

Key takeaways

  • Explicit reporting of item numbers is not very consistent. The number of items per condition is rarely explicitly mentioned, nor is the number of participants who see a given item. The choice of the number of items used is almost never explained or justified, e.g. via power analysis.
  • Detailed information about fillers is very rare. Studies often report the number of fillers, and some general descriptions, but almost none report why they chose that number (same goes for target items). Also, when item sets are provided, filler items are almost never included.

Item Reporting

Exact numbers of stimulus items can often be reconstructed / deduced from information in the article, with varying degrees of effort and certainty. But readers ideally should never be required to do this for themselves.

A surprising number of articles do not accurately report item numbers.

What was the total number of target items created for the study not including fillers or distractors?

There are 344 studies where the total number of items is not clear. Additionally we have 32 studies with 200 or more items. These need to be re-checked (the largest is 5^{4}!).

On the lower end, there are some studies that report semi-formal surveys of small sets of items, or even just one item, as in Boas (2011). These are often reported in footnotes with minimal details.

How many critical items are there per experimental condition?

The number of items per condition (min = 1, median = 12, max = 464) is an important aspect of study design, as it directly relates to the power of the study. Frustratingly, this is not always reported clearly.

We find 74 studies did not provide clear information about item numbers per condition.
